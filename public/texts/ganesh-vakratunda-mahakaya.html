Ganesh Invocation: Vakratunda Mahakaya

A short Sanskrit invocation to Ganesha, presented with the original verse, a pronunciation guide, and a plain-language meaning.

Updated 17 September 2026

On this page

This verse addresses Ganesha and asks that one's undertakings be free of obstacles. Its two lines bring together an image of the deity and a direct prayer.

Read the original text below, then use the pronunciation guide and meaning to follow the words. The explanation is an aid to understanding, rather than a prescription for religious practice.

पाठ · Original text

वक्रतुण्ड महाकाय

वक्रतुण्ड महाकाय कोटिसूर्यसमप्रभ । निर्विघ्नं कुरु मे देव सर्वकार्येषु सर्वदा ॥

Pronunciation guide

Vakratunda mahakaya koti-surya-sama-prabha,
Nirvighnam kuru me deva sarva-karyeshu sarvada.

This is a simple Roman reading aid. It does not capture every detail of Sanskrit pronunciation; listen to a knowledgeable reader or teacher when learning the recitation.

Meaning in plain language

O Lord with the curved trunk and great form, whose radiance is like that of ten million suns, make all my undertakings free of obstacles, always.

Vakratunda describes the curved trunk. Mahakaya refers to the great form. Koti-surya-sama-prabha describes radiance comparable to a multitude of suns. Nirvighnam asks for freedom from obstacles, while sarva-karyeshu sarvada extends the prayer to all undertakings, at all times.

Background & tradition

The verse appears as the opening invocation in the Veda-pada-stava section of the Brihat Stotra Ratnakara source linked below. This page presents that short invocation on its own, not the complete stava.

Some familiar recitations use surya-koti-sama-prabha in place of koti-surya-sama-prabha. The Sanskrit displayed here follows the word order of the cited printed source. Differences between recitations should be considered in the context of their source and tradition.

Reading notes

Pause at the end of each line and read at a comfortable pace. You can enlarge the original text, copy it, or print a reading copy using the controls on this page.

The verse expresses a devotional request. This page makes no promise of a particular outcome from recitation and does not set a required count, time, or ritual.
